# The salutation, the lineage, and the pledge to write

## 1 · Passage & Context

**Source Text:**

> ན་མོ་གུ་རུ་ཨཱརྱ་མཻ་ཏྲི་ཡེ། ཤེས་རབ་ཀྱི་ཕ་རོལ་ཏུ་ཕྱིན་པའི་མན་ངག་གི་བསྟན་བཅོས་མངོན་པར་རྟོགས་པའི་རྒྱན་འགྲེལ་པ་དང་བཅས་པའི་རྒྱ་ཆེར་ཤད་པ་ལེགས་བཤད་གསེར་གྱི་ཕྲེང་བ་ཞེས་བྱ་བ་དང་རྣམ་བཤད་སྙིང་པོའི་རྒྱན་ཅེས་བྱ་བ་གཉིས་ཀྱི་དཀའ་བའི་གནད་གསལ་བར་བྱེད་པ་ལེགས་བཤད་སྐལ་བཟང་ཀླུ་དབང་གི་རོལ་མཚོ་ཞེས་བྱ་བ། བླ་མ་དང་རྗེ་བཙུན་བྱམས་པ་མགོན་པོའི་ཞབས་ལ་གུས་པ་ཆེན་པོས་ཕྱག་འཚལ་ཞིང་སྐྱབས་སུ་མཆིའོ། ། བརྩེ་བ་ཆེན་པོས་དུས་ཐམས་ཅད་དུ་རྗེས་སུ་གཟུང་དུ་གསོལ་ལོ། ། རྣམ་མང་གདུལ་བྱའི་ཁམས་དང་བསམ་པ་ཀུན། ། རྣམ་ཀུན་མཁྱེན་པའི་སྤྱན་གྱིས་ལེགས་གཟིགས་ནས༑ ༑ རྣམ་གསུམ་ཆོས་ཀྱི་འཁོར་ལོ་བསྐོར་མཛད་པ། ། རྣམ་འདྲེན་ཐུབ་པའི་དབང་པོར་གུས་ཕྱག་འཚལ། ། … ལུགས་འདིའི་བརྒྱུད་པའི་བླ་མ་རྣམས་ལ་འདུད། ། རང་ཉིད་ཚེ་རབས་ཀུན་ཏུ་འདྲིས་ཕྱིར་དང་། ། རང་དང་སྐལ་མཉམ་གཞན་ལའང་ཕན་པའི་ཕྱིར། ། མདོ་རྒྱན་འགྲེལ་བའི་རྣམ་བཤད་རྣམ་གཉིས་ཀྱི༑ ༑ དཀའ་བའི་གནད་རྣམས་གསལ་བར་བྱེད་པ་ཡི། ། མདོ་དང་རྩ་འགྲེལ་སྦྱོར་བའི་ཆུ་གཏེར་ཆེ། ། ལེགས་བཤད་ཡིད་བཞིན་ནོར་བུས་རབ་ཏུ་གཏམ། ། སྐལ་བཟང་ཀླུ་དབང་དགའ་བའི་རྣམ་འགྱུར་གྱིས། ། རོལ་པའི་མཚོ་ཆེན་བདག་གིས་བྲི་བར་བྱ། །

(Section 2 gives the whole of it, unit by unit; the middle of the lineage list
is elided here only to keep this section readable.)

**Preceding Section:** p1, the title page. It gave the work's proper name and
said the volume is an extract (ལས) from a larger series, but it did not say
which two རྣམ་བཤད were meant. This paragraph does, in its first sentence.

**Following Section:** p3, where the treatise proper begins:
དེ་ལ་འདིར་བསྟན་བཅོས་མངོན་པར་རྟོགས་པའི་རྒྱན་འདི་འཆད་པ་ལ་… — the two-fold
division into the preliminaries and the treatise itself, and then the four
preliminaries, of which the first is identifying the root sūtra the *Ornament*
expounds.

**What this paragraph is.** Two conventional components, in the conventional
order: the **མཆོད་བརྗོད**, homage, running from ན་མོ to
ལུགས་འདིའི་བརྒྱུད་པའི་བླ་མ་རྣམས་ལ་འདུད, and the **རྩོམ་པར་དམ་བཅའ་བ**, the
pledge to compose, in the last three couplets. Conventional does not mean
empty. The homage is a *lineage list*, and a lineage list is a claim: it says
whose authority this book will invoke and, by omission, whose it will not.

## 2 · Translation & Breakdown

<!--line 0-27-->
> **ན་མོ་གུ་རུ་ཨཱརྱ་མཻ་ཏྲི་ཡེ།**
>
> *Namo guru Ārya-Maitreya* — homage to the guru, noble Maitreya.

<!--line 27-280-->
> **ཤེས་རབ་ཀྱི་ཕ་རོལ་ཏུ་ཕྱིན་པའི་མན་ངག་གི་བསྟན་བཅོས་མངོན་པར་རྟོགས་པའི་རྒྱན་འགྲེལ་པ་དང་བཅས་པའི་རྒྱ་ཆེར་ཤད་པ་ལེགས་བཤད་གསེར་གྱི་ཕྲེང་བ་ཞེས་བྱ་བ་དང་རྣམ་བཤད་སྙིང་པོའི་རྒྱན་ཅེས་བྱ་བ་གཉིས་ཀྱི་དཀའ་བའི་གནད་གསལ་བར་བྱེད་པ་ལེགས་བཤད་སྐལ་བཟང་ཀླུ་དབང་གི་རོལ་མཚོ་ཞེས་བྱ་བ།**
>
> *The Ocean of Play of Fortunate Nāga-Lords, of Good Explanation*, which
> clarifies the difficult points of two [works]: the *Golden Rosary of Good
> Explanation*, the extensive exposition of the *Ornament for Clear
> Realizations, Treatise of Instruction on the Perfection of Wisdom*, together
> with its commentary; and the *Ornament of the Essence, a Full Exposition*.

This is p1's title with the blank filled in. Where p1 said only
"the two རྣམ་བཤད," this names them: Tsongkhapa's ལེགས་བཤད་གསེར་གྱི་ཕྲེང་བ and
Gyaltsab Je's རྣམ་བཤད་སྙིང་པོའི་རྒྱན. Everything said about p1 depends on this
sentence.

<!--line 280-420-->
> **བླ་མ་དང་རྗེ་བཙུན་བྱམས་པ་མགོན་པོའི་ཞབས་ལ་གུས་པ་ཆེན་པོས་ཕྱག་འཚལ་ཞིང་སྐྱབས་སུ་མཆིའོ། ། བརྩེ་བ་ཆེན་པོས་དུས་ཐམས་ཅད་དུ་རྗེས་སུ་གཟུང་དུ་གསོལ་ལོ། །**
>
> At the feet of the guru and of the venerable protector Maitreya I bow with
> great reverence and go for refuge. I pray to be taken up, at all times, with
> great love.

<!--line 420-583-->
> **རྣམ་མང་གདུལ་བྱའི་ཁམས་དང་བསམ་པ་ཀུན། ། རྣམ་ཀུན་མཁྱེན་པའི་སྤྱན་གྱིས་ལེགས་གཟིགས་ནས༑ ༑ རྣམ་གསུམ་ཆོས་ཀྱི་འཁོར་ལོ་བསྐོར་མཛད་པ། ། རྣམ་འདྲེན་ཐུབ་པའི་དབང་པོར་གུས་ཕྱག་འཚལ། །**
>
> Having well seen, with the eye of the knowledge of all aspects, every
> constitution and disposition of the many kinds of disciple, he turned the
> three kinds of Dharma-wheel: to that guide, the lord of Sages, I bow with
> reverence.

All four lines open with རྣམ་, and the second is not decoration: the
Buddha sees with the eye of རྣམ་ཀུན་མཁྱེན་པ, *sarvākārajñatā*, which is the
subject of this very skabs.

<!--line 583-741-->
> **རྒྱལ་བ་ཀུན་གྱི་མཁྱེན་བརྩེ་གཅིག་བསྡུས་པ། ། རྒྱལ་སྲས་ཐུ་བོ་སེམས་དཔའི་ཚུལ་བཟུང་སྟེ། ། རྒྱལ་བའི་ཆོས་ཀྱི་སྲིད་ལ་དབང་བསྐུར་བ། ། རྒྱལ་ཚབ་དམ་པའི་ཞབས་ལ་གུས་པས་འདུད། །**
>
> Gathering into one the knowledge and love of all Conquerors, taking up the
> manner of a bodhisattva as eldest of the Conquerors' sons, consecrated to the
> sovereignty of the Conquerors' Dharma — at the feet of the holy Regent I bow
> with respect.

The four opening རྒྱལ་ answer the four རྣམ་ above. The Regent (རྒྱལ་ཚབ) is
Maitreya.

<!--line 741-897-->
> **གང་ལ་བརྟེན་ནས་ཡུམ་དོན་རྒྱ་མཚོ་ཡི། ། འཇུག་ངོགས་ཆེ་དེར་བདེ་བླག་དགྲོལ་བའི་གྲུ། ། རྒྱལ་ཡུམ་རྒྱན་འདི་གང་གི་ཐུགས་རྗེ་ཡིས། ། འགྲོ་ལ་སྣང་མཛད་འཕགས་པ་ཐོགས་མེད་དང་། །**
>
> Relying on which one is a ship easily launched at that great landing-place of
> the ocean of the Mother's meaning — this *Ornament of the Conquerors' Mother*,
> which through whose compassion was made to shine for beings: noble Asaṅga;
> and —

The ocean returns at the end of the paragraph as the book itself. Here it is
the ཡུམ་དོན, the meaning of the Mother; there it is what the author will write.

<!--line 897-1056-->
> **སངས་རྒྱས་གཉིས་པར་རབ་གྲགས་དབྱིག་གི་གཉེན། ། རབ་དགའི་ས་ཐོབ་འཕགས་པ་རྣམ་གྲོལ་སྡེ། ། དེ་ཡིས་རྗེས་བཟུང་བཙུན་པ་གྲོལ་སྡེ་དང་། ། མཆོག་སྡེ་འདུལ་བའི་སྡེ་དང་རྣམ་སྣང་མཛད། །**
>
> Vasubandhu, renowned as a second Buddha; noble Vimuktisena, who attained the
> Joyous Ground; Bhadanta Vimuktisena, taken up by him; and *Mchog sde*,
> *'Dul ba'i sde*, and Vairocana —

<!--line 1056-1209-->
> **སློབ་དཔོན་སེང་བཟང་སངས་རྒྱས་ཡེ་ཤེས་ཞབས༑ ༑ ཡོན་ཏན་བཤེས་གཉེན་ལོ་ཆེན་རིན་ཆེན་བཟང་། ། ཟླ་བ་བཟང་པོ་དྷི་ར་བ་ལ་དང་༑ ༑ བསྟན་སྐྱོང་འབུམ་ཕྲག་གསུམ་པ་རྔོག་ལོའི་ཞབས། །**
>
> Master Haribhadra, Buddhajñānapāda, Guṇamitra, the great translator Rinchen
> Zangpo, Candrabhadra, Dhīrapāla, Śāsanapāla, the *Master of the Three Hundred
> Thousand*, and Ngok Lotsāwa —

*Uncertain readings.* ཡོན་ཏན་བཤེས་གཉེན is not a name I can match to any work in
this corpus; see section 4. Whether བསྟན་སྐྱོང་ and འབུམ་ཕྲག་གསུམ་པ are two
masters or one master with an epithet is not determinable from the metre alone,
and I have not resolved it.

<!--line 1209-1448-->
> **འབྲེ་ཨར་གཞོན་ནུ་ཚུལ་ཁྲིམས་ཤེས་རབ་འབུམ། ། བྱང་ཆུབ་འབུམ་དང་གཉལ་ཞིག་འཇམ་རྡོར་དང་། ། བཟང་རིང་དར་ཚུལ་བརྩོན་འགྲུས་རྡོ་རྗེ་དང་། ། བོ་དོང་རིན་ཆེན་བརྩོན་འགྲུས་སེང་གེ་དང་། ། ཚད་མའི་སྐྱེས་བུ་བུ་སྟོན་ཐམས་ཅད་མཁྱེན། ། མཁས་པ་ཉ་དབོན་རྗེ་བཙུན་རེད་མདའ་བ། །**
>
> 'Bre, Ar, Zhönnu, Tshultrim, Sherab Bum, Byangchub Bum, Nyal-zhig Jamdor,
> Zangring, Dartsul, Tsöndrü Dorje, Bodong Rinchen Tsöndrü Sengge, the man of
> pramāṇa Butön the Omniscient, the learned Nya-ön, and the venerable Rendawa —

<!--line 1448-1521-->
> **ལ་སོགས་རྒྱ་བོད་མཁས་པའི་སྐྱེ་བོ་ལ། ། བདག་ཅག་སྒོ་གསུམ་གུས་པས་ཕྱག་འཚལ་ལོ༑ ༑**
>
> — and the rest: to the learned of India and Tibet we bow with the reverence
> of the three doors.

<!--line 1521-1672-->
> **ཁྱད་པར་ལུགས་འདིར་བཀའ་དྲིན་མཉམ་མེད་པའི། ། སྐད་གཉིས་སྨྲ་བ་ཀུན་གྱི་མཆོག་གྱུར་པ། ། རྔོག་ལོ་བསྟན་པའི་ཉི་མ་ཁུ་དབོན་ལ། ། སྒོ་གསུམ་གུས་པ་ཆེན་པོས་ཕྱག་འཚལ་ལོ། །**
>
> In particular, to those whose kindness in this system is unequalled, supreme
> among all speakers of the two tongues — Ngok Lotsāwa, sun of the teaching,
> uncle and nephew — I bow with the great reverence of the three doors.

> Conqueror's word and the learned speech that expounds its intent, and made
> clear in this Land of Snows the supreme path of liberation for beings — the

is addressed as the thunder-cloud whose burden is scripture, whose lightning is
reasoning, and whose thunder is emptiness.

## 3 · Structural Context (Dkar Chag)

- **Abhisamayālaṃkāra location:** p2 does have a counterpart here, and p1 did
  not. Maitreya's own text opens with a maṅgala verse of exactly this function:
  [ཉན་ཐོས་ཞི་བ་འཚོལ་རྣམས་ཀུན་ཤེས་ཉིད་ཀྱིས་ཉེར་ཞིར་ཁྲིད་མཛད་གང་ཡིན་དང་། … ཉན་ཐོས་བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔའི་ཚོགས་བཅས་སངས་རྒྱས་ཀྱི་ནི་ཡུམ་དེ་ལ་ཕྱག་འཚལ། །](quote:p2-q01)
  — homage to the Mother, articulated by the three knowledges (ཀུན་ཤེས,
  ལམ་ཤེས, རྣམ་པ་ཀུན་ལྡན). The two homages differ in a way worth marking:
  Maitreya salutes the **Mother** through the three knowledges, which are the
  architecture of his own text; Chökyi Gyaltsen salutes the **Teacher** through
  the three turnings of the wheel. Both are choices about what the following
  work is going to be about, and the second points at the first preliminary of
  p3 — identifying which sūtra, from which turning, the *Ornament* expounds.
- **Haribhadra's commentary location:** the འགྲེལ་པ་དོན་གསལ། opens with a
  མཆོད་བརྗོད that is not merely a homage but a *lineage*, and it is the same
  lineage in the same order:
  [འགྲོ་ལ་ཆགས་པས་ཀུན་ཆགས་མཛད། ། འཕགས་པ་ཐོགས་མེད་སྐྱོབ་ཉིད་ཀྱིས། ། མི་ཕམ་མགོན་ལ་གསན་ནས་ནི། ། བསྟན་བཅོས་ཆེན་པོའི་རྣམ་བཤད་མཛད། ། འགྲོ་དོན་རྩ་ལག་དབྱིག་གི་གཉེན།](quote:p2-q02)
  — Asaṅga hearing from Maitreya and making a རྣམ་བཤད, then Vasubandhu — and
  it continues to the two Vimuktisenas
  ([q03](quote:p2-q03)). p2's Indian list is Haribhadra's list, carried on past
  Haribhadra himself and into Tibet. This is the strongest structural fact
  about the paragraph, and it is not something the yig cha says; it becomes
  visible only when the two openings are read side by side.
- **Text location:** the second and last piece of front matter. Homage
  (མཆོད་བརྗོད) and pledge (རྩོམ་པར་དམ་བཅའ་བ), standing between the title page
  and the dkar chag that opens at p3.
- **Section type:** paratext — but with a doxographical payload, because a
  lineage list is an argument about descent.
- **Argumentative role:** it licenses the citations to come. When later
  paragraphs appeal to Haribhadra, to the two Vimuktisenas, to the gser phreng
  or the snying po'i rgyan, they are appealing to names already installed here
  as authorities. Equally, whom the list omits is a standing constraint.

## 4 · Philosophical Exposition & Commentary

**Core meaning.** Four movements. (i) Refuge and homage to guru and Maitreya,
in prose, the only prose in the paragraph. (ii) A descending homage in verse:
the Buddha, then Maitreya as Regent, then the Indian transmission from Asaṅga
to Ngok's Indian teachers, then the Tibetan transmission to Rendawa. (iii) Two
*singled-out* homages, breaking the list: one to Ngok Lotsāwa, one to
Tsongkhapa and the Gelug lineage. (iv) Motive and pledge. The shape is not
neutral: the two interruptions mark the two points at which this tradition
thinks the transmission was decisively re-founded in Tibet.

**The lineage is Haribhadra's, continued.** Haribhadra's maṅgala names Asaṅga,
Vasubandhu, Ārya Vimuktisena and Bhadanta Vimuktisena, and it *grades* them.
Ārya Vimuktisena, "though called Vimukti and belonging to the ārya class, saw
that what he had done was not done, and analysed with a mind abiding in the
Middle Way"; and then
[དེ་ཡི་འོག་ཏུ་རྣམ་གྲོལ་སྡེ། ། དད་པའི་ས་ལ་གནས་པ་ཡིས།](quote:p2-q03) — Bhadanta
Vimuktisena, "abiding at the stage of faith," made an exposition "in accord
with himself." p2 keeps the ranking — འཕགས་པ་རྣམ་གྲོལ་སྡེ has
རབ་དགའི་ས་ཐོབ, the Joyous Ground, while བཙུན་པ་གྲོལ་སྡེ is merely
དེ་ཡིས་རྗེས་བཟུང, "taken up by him" — and drops the criticism. That is what a
homage does to a polemic. A debater who knows only p2 will not know that
Haribhadra's own estimate of Bhadanta Vimuktisena is reserved.

**A census of the lineage against this corpus.** Of the masters p2 names, the
following are represented here by works on the *Ornament*, which is worth
setting out because it determines what a later paragraph can actually cite:

- **Vasubandhu (དབྱིག་གི་གཉེན)** — the ཡུམ་གསུམ་གནོད་འཇོམས།, which names itself
  in its own opening verse:
  [ཤེས་རབ་བདག་ཉིད་ཕ་རོལ་ཕྱིན་པའི་གཙོ། ། རྒྱལ་བའི་ཡུམ་ལ་གུས་པར་ཕྱག་འཚལ་ཏེ། ། བླ་མའི་ལུང་གིས་བདག་ལ་ཕན་པའི་ཕྱིར། ། འདི་ཡི་གཞུང་འགྲེལ་གནོད་འཇོམས་བྱ་བར་འདོད། །](quote:p2-q04).
  Note the name: གཞུང་འགྲེལ་གནོད་འཇོམས. This is the fourth of the four
  candidate "trail-blazers" that p6 will refuse, and the incidental question of
  p7 is precisely who composed it. Its author stands here in the homage
  lineage without qualification.
- **Ārya Vimuktisena (འཕགས་པ་རྣམ་གྲོལ་སྡེ)** — the ཉི་ཁྲི་སྣང་བ།, whose own
  head-title is
  [འཕགས་པ་ཤེས་རབ་ཀྱི་ཕ་རོལ་ཏུ་ཕྱིན་པ་སྟོང་ཕྲག་ཉི་ཤུ་ལྔ་པའི་མན་ངག་གི་བསྟན་བཅོས་མངོན་པར་རྟོགས་པའི་རྒྱན་གྱི་འགྲེལ་པ།](quote:p2-q05)
  — a commentary on the *Twenty-five Thousand* by way of the *Ornament*.
- **Bhadanta Vimuktisena (བཙུན་པ་གྲོལ་སྡེ)** — the
  [མངོན་པར་རྟོགས་པའི་རྒྱན་གྱི་ཚིག་ལེའུར་བྱས་པའི་རྣམ་པར་འགྲེལ་པ](quote:p2-q06). The
  titles record the difference of method the tradition remembers: the elder
  comments on the *sūtra* through the *Ornament*, the younger on the
  *kārikās*.
- **Haribhadra (སེང་བཟང)** — the འགྲེལ་པ་དོན་གསལ། and much else besides.
- **Buddhajñānapāda (སངས་རྒྱས་ཡེ་ཤེས་ཞབས)** — the
  [ཤེས་རབ་སྒྲོན་མའི་ཕྲེང་བ](quote:p2-q07).
- **Ngok Lotsāwa (རྔོག་ལོ)** — the ལུང་རིགས་ཀྱི་རོལ་མཚོ།.
- **Tsongkhapa, Gyaltsab Je, Khedrup Je** — the gser phreng, the snying po'i
  rgyan, and the ཕར་ཕྱིན་རྟོགས་དཀའི་སྣང་བ།.

Named but not represented here: Asaṅga, Rinchen Zangpo, Butön, Nya-ön,
Rendawa, Gendun Drub, and the whole Tibetan middle of the list. Represented
here but *not* named: Daṃṣṭrāsena, Kambala, Ratnākaraśānti, Abhayākaragupta,
Dharmamitra — and Dignāga. The last omission is not an accident of length. p6
will deny Dignāga's བརྒྱད་སྟོང་དོན་བསྡུས the status of an originating system on
the ground that it follows Asaṅga; a homage lineage that runs *through* Asaṅga
has no place for a second Asaṅgan branch, and p2 gives him none.

**One name I cannot place.** The verse reads ཡོན་ཏན་བཤེས་གཉེན between
Buddhajñānapāda and Rinchen Zangpo. The only བཤེས་གཉེན in this corpus with a
work on the *Ornament* is ཆོས་ཀྱི་བཤེས་གཉེན, Dharmamitra, whose
[འགྲེལ་བཤད་ཚིག་རབ་ཏུ་གསལ་བ](quote:p2-q08) is a ṭīkā on the kārikās. Whether
p2's name is a variant of his, or a different master, I cannot establish from
what is here, and I will not emend a name to make a list tidy. The same goes
for བསྟན་སྐྱོང་འབུམ་ཕྲག་གསུམ་པ, which may be one master or two, and for
དཔལ་འབྱོར་རྒྱལ་མཚན in the closing list, who appears nowhere in this corpus
outside this verse.

**Why Ngok gets a verse.** The ཁྱད་པར verse is the paragraph's strongest
statement, and its ground is visible on Ngok's own title page:

> [ཤེས་རབ་ཀྱི་ཕ་རོལ་ཏུ་ཕྱིན་པའི་མན་ངག་གི་བསྟན་བཅོས་མངོན་པར་རྟོགས་པའི་རྒྱན་འགྲེལ་པ་དང་བཅས་པའི་དཀའ་བའི་གནས་རྣམས་རྣམ་པར་བཤད་ནས་རང་གཞན་གྱི་གྲུབ་པའི་མཐའ་རྣམ་པར་དབྱེ་བ་ལུང་རིགས་ཀྱི་རོལ་མཚོ་བཞུགས་སོ།།](quote:p2-q09)

Base object, task, and the ocean-image of the proper name are all already
there; p1's title differs from it by one storey. So ལུགས་འདིར — "in *this*
system" — is exact rather than pious: Ngok founded the form of study this book
is an instance of. Note also that Ngok's own salutation is
[ན་མོ་མཉྫུ་ཤྲཱི་ཡེ།](quote:p2-q10), to Mañjuśrī, where p2's is to Ārya
Maitreya. Both are conventional; the choice still says something. Ngok's is
the scholar's dedication to wisdom itself, Chökyi Gyaltsen's is to the author
of the text he is about to expound.

*My own knowledge, not textual support:* ཁུ་དབོན, "uncle and nephew," is the
standard designation of the two Ngoks, Lekpai Sherab and his nephew Loden
Sherab, and it is Loden Sherab whose work stands in this corpus. Likewise
'Bre and Ar in the following verse are ordinarily identified as two of Ngok
Loden Sherab's principal disciples, and Nyal-zhig Jampai Dorje, Bodong Rinchen
Tsöndrü Sengge, Butön, Nya-ön Kunga Pal and Rendawa Zhönnu Lodrö as the
Tibetan Prajñāpāramitā line leading to Tsongkhapa. None of that is established
by anything I have found in these files, and a reader who wants it grounded
should look outside them.

**Tsongkhapa's verse, and what it withholds.** Tsongkhapa gets a whole verse,
like Ngok, and unlike Gyaltsab and Khedrup, who get a line each. But the
praise is of his *method* — scripture borne, reasoning flashing, emptiness
sounding — not of any work. The two works the title page depends on are named
in the opening prose sentence and nowhere in the verse. The homage honours the
men; the title-sentence names the books; the paragraph keeps the two functions
apart.

**The immediate lineage.** The closing list moves from Gyaltsab to the author's
own teachers, and one link can be confirmed. The hagiography records that
[དེ་དུས་ཀུན་མཁྱེན་ཆོས་འབྱོར་དཔལ་བཟང་ཀློག་སློབ་པས་སློབ་དཔོན་གྱི་གཙོ་བོར](quote:p2-q11)
— Kunkhyen Chos-jor Palzang was his chief teacher — which fixes p2's
ཀུན་མཁྱེན་གཉིས་པ་ཆོས་འབྱོར་དཔལ་བཟང. For the last name, བདེ་ལེགས་མཚན་ཅན, the
likeliest referent is the master from whom he took his first vows at seven,
[གནས་རྙིང་ཆོས་རྗེ་ཀུན་དགའ་བདེ་ལེགས་རིན་ཆེན་རྒྱལ་མཚན་དཔལ་བཟང་པོ](quote:p2-q12),
of whom the same source says
[སྔ་ཕྱིར་བཀའ་དྲིན་ཚད་མེད་པས་བསྐྱངས་པས་བླ་མ་ཡིན་པས་བླ་མའི་གཙོ་བོ་ཞིག་ཏུ་མཛད](quote:p2-q13)
— "because he had cared for him with immeasurable kindness early and late, he
made him a chief among his lamas." The match of བཀའ་དྲིན is suggestive and I
give it as probable, not settled: "Delek" is a common element in names, and the
list is a lineage of *this system* rather than a list of personal teachers.

**A note on the evidence.** The structural claims here — that p2's lineage is
Haribhadra's continued, that the Indian masters named are the ones with works
in this corpus, that Dignāga is missing — rest on tier-3 and tier-4 primary
texts and are as solid as the corpus allows. Everything about the Tibetan
middle of the list rests on nothing at all in these files, and everything about
the closing list rests on a single tier-5 hagiography. The paragraph is a good
example of a passage where the evidence is thickest at the ends and thinnest in
the middle, and it would be dishonest to write it up at a uniform confidence.

## 5 · Bones of Contention 🦴

**What is disputed?** Whose transmission a Prajñāpāramitā yig cha stands in —
and specifically, whether the lineage of the *Ornament* is a self-sufficient
line of descent or a branch that must be joined to Nāgārjuna's before it can
claim to deliver the sūtra's meaning.

**Who argues what?**

- **p2 as it stands.** The list runs Buddha → Maitreya → Asaṅga → Vasubandhu →
  the Vimuktisenas → Haribhadra → the transmission to Tibet. **Nāgārjuna is not
  in it.** Neither are the Six Collections. This is the Maitreya line and only
  the Maitreya line.
- **p6, four paragraphs later.** There Chökyi Gyaltsen will hold that the
  systems which genuinely *open* a way for Prajñāpāramitā exegesis are exactly
  two, Nāgārjuna's Six Collections and Maitreya's *Ornament*. On that account,
  a homage that names only one of the two is naming half the field.
- **Haribhadra, whom p2 is following.** His maṅgala too begins from Maitreya
  and Asaṅga ([q02](quote:p2-q02)) — and yet he characterises Ārya
  Vimuktisena's achievement as analysing "with a mind abiding in the Middle
  Way" (དབུ་མར་གནས་པའི་བློས་རྣམ་ཕྱེ།, [q03](quote:p2-q03)). The Madhyamaka is
  present in the Indian lineage as a *qualification of how the Ornament is
  read*, not as a second line of descent.

**Why does it matter?** Because the Gelug reading of the *Ornament* is that it
sets out the hidden meaning (སྦས་དོན) of sūtras whose explicit meaning is
emptiness as Nāgārjuna expounds it. If that is right, the Ornament's lineage
cannot be doctrinally self-sufficient, and a homage that stops at Maitreya
looks like it is claiming more independence for this line than the tradition
allows. If, on the other hand, a མཆོད་བརྗོད is simply a record of *by whom this
text was handed down*, then Nāgārjuna's absence is a fact about textual
transmission and carries no doctrinal weight at all.

**Resolution.** The second reading is the right one, and the paragraph itself
supplies the reason: the lineage is introduced by
གང་ལ་བརྟེན་ནས་ཡུམ་དོན་རྒྱ་མཚོ་ཡི་… རྒྱལ་ཡུམ་རྒྱན་འདི་གང་གི་ཐུགས་རྗེ་ཡིས་འགྲོ་ལ་སྣང་མཛད།
— these are the masters through whom *this Ornament* was made to shine. It is a
descent list for one book, not a doxography. Haribhadra's own practice supports
this: he too gives no separate Madhyamaka line, and instead builds the
Madhyamaka into how the line is to be read. Sera Jey therefore takes the
absence of Nāgārjuna here as saying nothing against p6, and I know of no
Gelug house that reads it otherwise; but a debater should be able to say *why*
it says nothing, and the reason is the relative clause in the third verse and
not merely convention.

**A second, smaller bone.** Whether Bhadanta Vimuktisena is to be honoured as
an authority or discounted as a lesser witness. Haribhadra says he stood only
at the stage of faith and expounded "in accord with himself"; p2 puts him in
the homage with no such reservation. Where a later paragraph of this yig cha
takes a position against Bhadanta Vimuktisena, it is Haribhadra's estimate and
not p2's that will be doing the work, and it is worth knowing in advance that
the two are not saying the same thing.

## 6 · Key Terminology & Technical Concepts

- **མཆོད་བརྗོད། / རྩོམ་པར་དམ་བཅའ་བ།** — homage, and pledge to compose.
  - **Sanskrit equivalent:** *maṅgala* / *pratijñā*.
  - **How understood here:** the pair is a genre requirement, but the *content*
    of each is free. What Chökyi Gyaltsen puts into the homage is a
    transmission list, and what he puts into the pledge is a restatement of his
    title — so the two conventional slots are used to fix, respectively, his
    authorities and his object.

- **རྒྱལ་ཚབ།** — regent, vice-regent of the Conqueror.
  - **How understood here:** Maitreya, in the second verse. Ten verses later the
    same word begins the name རྒྱལ་ཚབ་དར་མ. The recurrence is not a pun the
    text draws attention to, but a reader following the argument of the volume
    should notice that the title given to Maitreya at the head of the Indian
    lineage is the name of the man who wrote one of the two expositions the
    book is about.

- **རྣམ་ཀུན་མཁྱེན་པ།** — the knowledge of all aspects.
  - **Sanskrit equivalent:** *sarvākārajñatā*.
  - **How understood here:** in the first verse it is the Buddha's *eye*, the
    faculty by which he assessed disciples before turning the wheel. That is a
    functional use, not the technical definition that skabs dang po will spend
    itself establishing, and the shift from the one to the other is precisely
    what the first chapter is for.
  - **Debates:** whether the term in such a verse is being used in its strict
    sense is not a live dispute; but the practice of opening the first skabs
    with the first skabs' own subject-term is worth registering as deliberate.

- **རྣམ་གསུམ་ཆོས་ཀྱི་འཁོར་ལོ།** — the three turnings of the Dharma-wheel.
  - **How understood here:** the frame within which the first preliminary of p3
    operates. To ask which sūtra the *Ornament* expounds is to ask which
    turning it belongs to, and this verse has already placed all three turnings
    under one intention.

- **ལུགས་འདི།** — "this system."
  - **How understood here:** used twice, and not of the same thing. In the Ngok
    verse it means the Tibetan tradition of studying the *Ornament together with
    its commentary* by way of its difficult points — the genre. In the closing
    line, ལུགས་འདིའི་བརྒྱུད་པའི་བླ་མ, it means the Gelug transmission of that
    study. The paragraph does not mark the shift, and a reader who does not
    notice it will over-read the first occurrence.

- **སྐད་གཉིས་སྨྲ་བ།** — "speaker of the two tongues," i.e. a translator.
  - **How understood here:** an honorific with an argumentative edge. Ngok is
    praised not as a commentator but as the man who could check the Tibetan
    against the Sanskrit — which is the specific competence a tradition of
    difficult-point exegesis most needs and least often has.

- **ཁུ་དབོན།** — uncle and nephew, as a joint designation.
  - **How understood here:** it makes the homage fall on a *pair* rather than a
    person, which is how the Ngok transmission is remembered. This is the
    standard identification and I give it as my own knowledge; the corpus here
    contains a work by only one of the two.
