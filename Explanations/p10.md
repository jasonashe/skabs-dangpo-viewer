A ཞར་བྱུང, an incidental question raised by the previous paragraph. The

## 2 · Two works of the same name

> **འདི་ལ་ཡུམ་གསུམ་གྱི་གཞུང་འགྲེལ་གནོད་འཇོམས་ཞེས་པ་ཡེ་ཤེས་སྡེས་བསྒྱུར་བ་ཞིག་དང་། འབུམ་རྐྱང་པའི་གཞུང་འགྲེལ་གནོད་འཇོམས་ཞེས་པ་འགྱུར་བྱང་མེད་པ་ཞིག་ཡོད་པའི་ཕྱི་མ་རྒྱལ་པོ་ཁྲི་སྲོང་ལྡེ་བཙན་གྱིས་མཛད་པ་ཡིན་ཏེ།**
>
> There are two: a *Gnod 'joms* on the three mothers, translated by Yeshe Dé,
> and a *Gnod 'joms* on the 100,000 alone, without a translator's colophon. The
> latter was composed by King Trisong Detsen.

**The move.** The confusion the paragraph is resolving is a confusion of two
books. One is an Indian work with a Tibetan translator on record; the other has
no འགྱུར་བྱང at all — and the absence of a translator's colophon is itself
evidence, because a work needs no translator if it was composed in Tibetan.

## 3 · The warrant

> **འཕང་ཐང་གི་དཀར་ཆག་ཏུ། [བཙན་པོ་ཁྲི་སྲོང་ལྡེ་བཙན་གྱིས་མཛད་པའི་འཕགས་པ་ཤེས་རབ་ཀྱི་ཕ་རོལ་ཏུ་ཕྱིན་པ་འབུམ་གྱི་རྒྱ་ཆེར་འགྲེལ།](quote:q1001) ཞེས་གསུངས་པའི་ཕྱིར།**
>
> Because the *'Phang thang Catalogue* says: "The *Extensive Commentary on the
> Noble Perfection of Wisdom in One Hundred Thousand Lines*, composed by the
> Emperor Trisong Detsen." — because that is what is said.

The 'Phang thang ma is one of the imperial-period catalogues of the Tibetan
canon, compiled at the 'Phang thang palace. Citing a catalogue rather than a
doctrinal authority is exactly right for a question of authorship, and it is
worth noting that Tsongkhapa cites the same entry in the
ལེགས་བཤད་གསེར་ཕྲེང — this is not a Sera Jey particularity but the received
Gelug reading.

## 4 · The unresolved half

> **དང་པོ་དེ་ལ་མི་མཐུན་པ་མང་དུ་འབྱུང་ཡང་། སྔ་མ་རྣམས་ལ་ཁ་ཆེ་མཆེ་བའི་སྡེས་མཛད་པར་གྲགས་པ་ལྟར་ཡིན་ནམ་སྙམ་སྟེ་དཔྱད་པར་བྱའོ། །**
>
> As for the former, although much disagreement arises about it, I think it may
> be as the earlier masters held — that it was composed by Daṃṣṭrasena of
> Kashmir. This should be examined.

**Read the register.** ཡིན་ནམ་སྙམ་སྟེ་དཔྱད་པར་བྱའོ is not a conclusion. It is
the formula for an opinion offered without a proof, flagged as such and left
open. A debater may not be held to it, and citing it as the Jetsunpa position
is a misuse of the text.

**Bones of contention.** 🦴 The disputed point is the authorship of the
three-mother *Gnod 'joms*. The tradition ascribes it to ཁ་ཆེ་མཆེ་བའི་སྡེ
(Daṃṣṭrasena), and the Derge Tengyur carries it under that name; but the
attribution is contested, and Jetsun Chökyi Gyaltsen declines to settle it.
That he settles the *Tibetan* work's authorship by catalogue and leaves the
*Indian* work's open is a nice illustration of how evidence is weighted here:
documentary record decides, reputation does not.

## 5 · Key terms

- **འགྱུར་བྱང་།** — translator's colophon. Its presence or absence is
  bibliographic evidence of a work's origin, used as such here.
- **དཀར་ཆག** — catalogue. The 'Phang thang ma and the lHan kar ma are the two
  imperial catalogues, and both are treated in this literature as
  documentary authorities on what existed and who wrote it.
- **དཔྱད་པར་བྱའོ།** — "this should be examined": an explicit refusal to
  decide. Distinguish it carefully from ཤེས་པར་བྱའོ ("this should be known"),
  which asserts.
