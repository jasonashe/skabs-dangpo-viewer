## 2 · The remaining nine

> **དེ་ནས་བརྒྱ་བྱིན་དང་། དེ་ནས་རབ་འབྱོར་དང་། བྱམས་པ་དང་། རབ་འབྱོར་དང་། བརྒྱ་བྱིན་དང་། རབ་འབྱོར་དང་། བྱམས་པ་དང་། རབ་འབྱོར་དང་། དེ་ནས་ཀུན་དགའ་བོ་ལ་བཀའ་སྩལ་ནས་ཡོངས་སུ་གཏད་པའོ། །**
>
> Then Śakra; then Subhūti; then Maitreya; then Subhūti; then Śakra; then
> Subhūti; then Maitreya; then Subhūti; and then the entrustment, addressed to
> Ānanda.

In order, the eleven interlocutors are:

1. Śāriputra — addressed by the Buddha
2. Subhūti — the extensive explanation
3. Śakra (བརྒྱ་བྱིན)
4. Subhūti
5. Maitreya
6. Subhūti
7. Śakra
8. Subhūti
9. Maitreya
10. Subhūti
11. Ānanda — the entrustment

## 3 · Philosophical exposition

**Why this counts as a division.** A round (རྣམ་གྲངས) is marked by a change of
interlocutor, not of topic. The division is therefore a *formal* one — it can be
read off the sūtra without any doctrinal judgement, which is exactly what makes
it useful as a way of locating passages. The Ornament's eight abhisamayas, by
contrast, are a division by content and require interpretation to apply.

**The shape of the list.** Subhūti holds five of the eleven, and they alternate
with the others: 2, 4, 6, 8, 10. The sūtra returns to him each time a divine or
bodhisattva interlocutor has spoken. Since Subhūti is the Buddha's
foremost disciple in the realization of emptiness, the alternation is doing
work: each excursus is brought back to the one who can state the ultimate
directly.

**The frame.** The first round is addressed to Śāriputra — the foremost in
wisdom of the analytic kind — and the last to Ānanda, the foremost in
retention. The mother sūtras open with understanding and close with
transmission. The entrustment to Ānanda is the passage quoted at p6 as the
second proof that the Prajñāpāramitā is the deepest of the teachings.

## 4 · Key terms

- **རྣམ་གྲངས།** — round, enumeration-member. Here a stretch of the sūtra marked
  off by who is being addressed.
- **ཡོངས་སུ་གཏད་པ།** — entrustment. The closing act by which a sūtra is
  committed to a custodian; in the mother sūtras, to Ānanda.
- **བརྒྱ་བྱིན།** — Śakra, lord of the Thirty-Three. His questions in the mother
  sūtras concern the merit of the perfection of wisdom rather than its nature,
  which is why his rounds sit between Subhūti's.
