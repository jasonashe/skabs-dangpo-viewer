# Which traditions open a way? — the four-fold claim refuted

## 1 · Passage & Context

**Source Text:**

> གཉིས་པ་ལ་ཁ་ཅིག ། ཤེར་ཕྱིན་གྱི་མདོའི་དགོངས་འགྲེལ་དུ་གྱུར་པའི་ཤིང་རྟའི་སྲོལ་འབྱེད་དེ་ལ་བཞིར་ཡོད་དེ། རིགས་ཚོགས་དྲུག ། མངོན་རྟོགས་རྒྱན། བརྒྱད་སྟོང་དོན་བསྡུས། གཞུང་འགྲེལ་གནོད་འཇོམས་རྣམས་སུ་ཡོད་པའི་ཕྱིར། ཞེས་ཟེར།

**Preceding Section:** p5, where Jetsun Chökyi Gyaltsen set down his own
position (རང་ལུགས) that the three mothers — རྒྱས་འབྲིང་བསྡུས — are the root
sūtra the *Ornament* expounds. That closed the **first** of the four
preliminaries listed at p3.

**Following Section:** p7, an incidental question (ཞར་བྱུང) about who composed
the གཞུང་འགྲེལ་གནོད་འཇོམས།, raised precisely because this paragraph has just
assigned that work a place in Nāgārjuna's line.

**Where "the second" points.** གཉིས་པ is the second of the four preliminaries
of p3: རྒྱན་གྱིས་མདོ་དོན་ཇི་ལྟར་བཀྲལ་བའི་ཚུལ, the manner in which the
*Ornament* construes the sūtra's meaning. In practice the question is one of
lineage — which Indian works count as ཤིང་རྟའི་སྲོལ་འབྱེད, trail-blazers, for
Prajñāpāramitā exegesis.

## 3 · Structural Context (Dkar Chag)

- **Abhisamayālaṃkāra location:** outside the root verses. This belongs to the
  Tibetan commentarial frame built *around* Maitreya's text — the survey of who
  has expounded the sūtra and by what method — not to any of the eight
  abhisamayas.
- **Haribhadra's commentary location:** correspondingly, this has no lemma in
  the འགྲེལ་པ་དོན་གསལ།. Haribhadra opens by identifying the object of
  exposition and moves to the eight subjects; the question of *rival* Indian
  systems is a Tibetan doxographical addition. Saying so is itself a finding:
  the paragraph is doing work the Indian commentaries did not need to do.
- **Text location:** སྐབས་དང་པོའི་སྤྱི་དོན།, first of the four preliminaries'
  four sub-topics — specifically the **second**,
  རྒྱན་གྱིས་མདོ་དོན་ཇི་ལྟར་བཀྲལ་བའི་ཚུལ.
- **Section type:** objection and refutation (གཞན་ལུགས་དགག་པ), followed by an
  excursus on Dignāga's thirty-two topics.
- **Argumentative role:** it cuts the field of Indian Prajñāpāramitā systems
  from four to two. Everything downstream in the yig cha depends on the result:
  if the *Ornament* is one of exactly two trail-blazing systems, then following
  it is a choice between two, not one among four.

## 4 · Philosophical Exposition & Commentary

**Core meaning.** To be a ཤིང་རྟའི་སྲོལ་འབྱེད — literally, one who opens the
chariot-track — is to *originate* a system of exegesis, not to travel one
already opened. The refutation does not deny that Dignāga's *Summary* and the
*Gnod 'joms* expound the sūtra; it denies that they do so on their own
authority. Dignāga walks behind Asaṅga, the *Gnod 'joms* behind Nāgārjuna. A
follower cannot be a trail-blazer, so the count of four collapses to two:
Nāgārjuna's Six Collections and Maitreya's *Ornament*.

**Logical reasoning.** Notice the *kind* of evidence used for each limb, because
it is the same kind twice and it is not testimony.

- For Dignāga, the evidence is **doctrinal content**. The verse quoted reads
  the sūtra through the three natures and treats its negations as negations of
  the imputed only. That is the Yogācāra hermeneutic; a text that uses it is
  working inside Asaṅga's system whatever it says about itself.
- For the *Gnod 'joms*, the evidence is **citation habit**. A commentary that
  reaches for the རྩ་ཤེ whenever it must explain emptiness has told you whose
  framework it is in.

Neither limb asks anyone's opinion about who follows whom. Both read the
affiliation off the page. This is characteristic of how the yig cha settles
questions of lineage, and it is worth pointing out to a debater who expects an
appeal to authority here.

**Je Tsongkhapa's perspective.** The ལེགས་བཤད་གསེར་ཕྲེང treats the *Gnod 'joms*
at length in the same connection, [discussing its reading of how long the
teaching endures and noting where Śāntarakṣita's intention appears to conflict
with the ṭīkā](quote:p6-q01). Tsongkhapa's concern there is the work's
*content*; Chökyi Gyaltsen's here is its *affiliation*. They are compatible,
and the Sera Jey presentation assumes the reader has the gser phreng discussion
in the background.

**Drepung comparison.** Jamyang Zhepa, in the
ཕར་ཕྱིན་མཐའ་དཔྱོད་རིན་ཆེན་སྒྲོན་མེ།, reaches for the gser phreng on precisely
this paragraph's second limb: [the Gzhung 'grel, in explaining the sixteen
emptinesses and non-arising, cites the Rtsa she](quote:p6-q02). So Gomang and
Sera Jey do not part company on the evidence — both read the *Gnod 'joms*'s
citation habit as fixing its affiliation. What this paragraph adds is the step
from that habit to the conclusion that the work is therefore not a
trail-blazer.

**Sera Jey specificity.** The reduction to two — ཀླུ་སྒྲུབ and བྱམས་པ — is the
standard Gelug position and not distinctive to Sera Jey. What is distinctive
here is the compression: Chökyi Gyaltsen disposes of both candidates in a
single ཕྱིར clause each and then spends the rest of the paragraph on Dignāga's
thirty-two topics, a scheme he has just declined to treat as a rival. That is a
deliberate courtesy — the *Summary* is demoted as a system and immediately
honoured as an analysis.

**A note on the evidence.** The verified quotes available for this paragraph
are thin at the top: nothing from Chökyi Gyaltsen's own corpus and nothing from
Gyaltsab Je bears directly on it, and the strongest witness is tier 2
(Tsongkhapa). The reading given above for the two limbs rests on the yig cha's
own words rather than on corroborating commentary, which is appropriate for a
passage that is itself doing doxographical work — but it should be said rather
than disguised.

## 5 · Bones of Contention 🦴

**What is disputed?** How many Indian systems genuinely originate a reading of
the Prajñāpāramitā sūtras — four, or two?

**Who argues what?**

- **The ཁ་ཅིག position:** four. The Six Collections, the *Ornament*, Dignāga's
  *Summary*, and the *Gnod 'joms*. The appeal is to the fact that each of the
  four does expound the sūtra's intent.
- **Jetsun Chökyi Gyaltsen:** two. Expounding the intent is necessary but not
  sufficient; originating the method is what the term names.

**Why does it matter?** Two consequences, one narrow and one wide.

Narrowly, it fixes what the fourth preliminary can be asking. If four systems
were live, "which commentary do we follow?" would be a choice among four
methods; with two, the question at p18 — Haribhadra's འགྲེལ་པ་དོན་གསལ། — is a
choice of commentator *within* Maitreya's system.

Widely, it protects the sense in which the *Ornament* is a
དགོངས་འགྲེལ. If a Yogācāra reading of the sūtra were an independent
trail-blazing system rather than a derivative one, the Prajñāpāramitā would have
two equally original Indian construals and the Gelug claim that the Ornament
sets out the *definitive* intent would need much more defending than this
paragraph gives it.

**Resolution.** Sera Jey follows the two-system reading, on the ground that
ཤིང་རྟའི་སྲོལ་འབྱེད is a term about origination and that the affiliation of
each disputed text can be read from its own method. The refutation is not that
Dignāga is wrong, but that he is Asaṅga's.

## 6 · Key Terminology & Technical Concepts

- **ཤིང་རྟའི་སྲོལ་འབྱེད།** — trail-blazer, "opener of the chariot-track."
  - **Sanskrit equivalent:** roughly *mārga-praṇetṛ* / the "chariot-way"
    imagery behind शास्त्र-प्रणेतृ.
  - **How understood here:** strictly as *origination*. This is the whole hinge
    of the paragraph: read it loosely as "one who expounds authoritatively" and
    the four-fold count stands.
  - **Debates:** the looser reading is exactly what the ཁ་ཅིག position needs,
    and it is not absurd; the text does not argue for the strict sense, it
    assumes it.

- **དགོངས་འགྲེལ།** — commentary on the intent, as against commentary on the
  words (ཚིག་འགྲེལ).
  - **How understood here:** the question is who has expounded the sūtra's
    *intent*, which is why doctrinal framework and not literal glossing is the
    criterion of affiliation.

- **ངོ་བོ་ཉིད་གསུམ།** — the three natures.
  - **Sanskrit equivalent:** *trisvabhāva*.
  - **How understood here:** cited not as a doctrine to be assessed but as a
    *fingerprint*. Its presence in the *Summary* identifies the school; whether
    the three-nature reading of the sūtra is true is not at issue in this
    paragraph.

- **དོན་ཚན་སོ་གཉིས།** — the thirty-two topics.
  - **How understood here:** Dignāga's way of carving the Eight Thousand,
    parallel in function to Maitreya's eight abhisamayas and, on the surface, in
    competition with them. The paragraph's structure answers that: having denied
    the *Summary* the status of a rival system, it can set out the thirty-two
    without embarrassment.
  - **Debates:** how the count reaches thirty-two (16 + 10 + 6) is stated here
    without argument; the ten antidotes to the ten distractions are the item a
    reader is most likely to want unpacked, and the paragraph does not unpack
    them.
