# The author's own position: the three mothers are the root sūtra

**PDF pages 40–41 · folio 5 · སྐབས་དང་པོའི་སྤྱི་དོན།**

<!--line 0-12-->
## 1 · Passage and context

**Preceding section:** p7, where both the seventeen-fold and the nine-fold
enumerations were refuted.

**Following section:** p9, the second of the four preliminaries — how the
*Ornament* construes the sūtra's meaning, and which traditions of exegesis
count as ཤིང་རྟའི་སྲོལ་འབྱེད.

> **རང་ལུགས་ནི།**
>
> Our own position:

The paragraph is short, and that is the point. Having spent a folio and a half
dismantling two counts, Jetsun Chökyi Gyaltsen states his own in a single
sentence and supports it with two citations. This is the shape of a
རང་ལུགས་བཞག་པ passage throughout the yig cha: refutation is long, position is
short, and the weight is carried by scripture.

## 2 · The position

> **ཡུམ་རྒྱས་འབྲིང་བསྡུས་གསུམ་པོ་འདི་ཉིད་འདིའི་བཤད་བྱ་རྩ་བའི་མདོ་ཡིན་ཏེ།**
>
> These very three — the extensive, the middling and the condensed mother — are
> the root sūtra expounded here.

The three are the འབུམ (100,000), the ཉི་ཁྲི (25,000) and the
ཁྲི་བརྒྱད་སྟོང་པ (18,000). Notice what this does to the previous dispute: it
does not offer a *better* enumeration of all Prajñāpāramitā sūtras. It changes
the question. The issue is not how many Prajñāpāramitā sūtras exist, but which
of them the *Ornament* is a commentary on — and the answer is the three that
set out all eight abhisamayas at length. The Verse Summary problem simply does
not arise, because the Verse Summary is not among the three.

## 3 · First warrant — Haribhadra

> **འགྲེལ་པར། [རྒྱལ་བའི་ཡུམ་རྣམ་པ་གསུམ་ཆར་ལས་ཀྱང་བརྗོད་པར་བྱ་བ་ཉིད་དུ་བསྟན་པ།](quote:q0801) ཞེས་དང་།**
>
> In the *Commentary*: "what is taught as the subject matter in all three
> mothers of the Conquerors" — thus;

**Argumentative role.** "འགྲེལ་པ" without qualification means Haribhadra's
འགྲེལ་པ་དོན་གསལ།, the commentary this text follows (settled at p20). The line
is his gloss on the *Ornament*'s statement of its own subject matter, and it
says "all three" — not one, not seventeen. For the Sera Jey reading this
single phrase is decisive, because the authority that fixes the Ornament's
object of exposition should be the commentary the Ornament is being read
through.

Panchen Sonam Drakpa reads the same lemma in his
[འགྲེལ་ཚིག་དོན་རབ་གསལ།](quote:q0802) and reaches the same three; the Loseling
and Sera Jey textbooks do not part company here, which is worth knowing —
not every disagreement in the first chapter is a house disagreement.

## 4 · Second warrant — the verse

> **ལ་ལ་ལས་དོན་གཞན་ལས་ཚིག ། གཞན་ལས་རྣམ་གྲངས་མིང་གིས་ཏེ། ། མཁས་པ་རྣམས་ཀྱིས་ཡུམ་ཀུན་ལས། ། དོན་བསྡུས་དེ་ཙམ་ཤེས་པར་བྱ། ། ཞེས་གསུངས་པའི་ཕྱིར།**
>
> "From one, the meaning; from another, the words; from another, the synonyms
> and names — the wise should know that just this much is drawn together from
> all the mothers." Because that is what is said.

**Core meaning.** The verse describes a method of composition, and the method
presupposes plurality. If the *Ornament* draws its meaning from one mother, its
wording from another and its terminology from another, then it cannot have a
single mother as its root sūtra. The verse and Haribhadra's phrase are
therefore not two witnesses to the same fact; they establish different halves
of the position — Haribhadra that it is *all three*, and the verse *why* it must
be more than one.

## 5 · Bones of contention 🦴

**What is disputed?** Whether "the root sūtra of the *Ornament*" names one text
or several.

**Positions.** The seventeen-fold and nine-fold counts (p7) both answer a
question about the Prajñāpāramitā corpus as a whole and then treat that answer
as if it settled the Ornament's object of exposition. Jetsun Chökyi Gyaltsen
separates the two questions. Whether the corpus contains seventeen sūtras or
nine or some other number is left open here — the text's own last word on the
seventeen was that it is both too few and too many, which is a refutation, not
a replacement.

**Why it matters.** The three-mother answer is what allows the eight
abhisamayas to be read as the structure of a *class* of sūtras rather than of
one particular text, and so what allows the Ornament to function as a
commentarial key rather than a paraphrase.

## 6 · Key terms

- **རང་ལུགས།** — one's own system, as against གཞན་ལུགས (another's). A
  རང་ལུགས passage is answerable; the positions refuted before it are not the
  author's and need not be made consistent with anything else he says.
- **བཤད་བྱ་རྩ་བའི་མདོ།** — the root sūtra to be expounded. "Root" here is
  relative to the commentary, not absolute: it names what a given treatise
  comments on.
- **རྒྱས་འབྲིང་བསྡུས།** — extensive, middling, condensed. A standard triad
  applied to the mothers; the same triad is what the nine-fold count of p7
  squared, with the results shown there.
