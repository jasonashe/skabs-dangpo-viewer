# The three gates by which the Gnod 'joms expounds the mother

## 1 · Passage and context

**Preceding section:** p10, on the authorship of the *Gnod 'joms*.

**Following section:** p12, which lists the eleven rounds (རྣམ་གྲངས་བཅུ་གཅིག)
announced at the end of this paragraph.

> **དེས་ན་ཡུམ་རྒྱས་འབྲིང་གཉིས་ཁྲི་བརྒྱད་སྟོང་པ་དང་གསུམ་གྱི་རྒྱ་ཆེར་བཤད་པ་གཞུང་འགྲེལ་གནོད་འཇོམས་ཀྱིས་སྒོ་གསུམ་དང་རྣམ་གྲངས་བཅུ་གཅིག་གི་སྒོ་ནས་ཡུམ་འགྲེལ་བར་བྱེད་པ་ཡིན་ལ།**
>
> Therefore the *Gnod 'joms*, the extensive explanation of the three — the
> extensive and middling mothers and the 18,000 — expounds the mother by way of
> three gates and eleven rounds.

Note that the *Gnod 'joms* comments on exactly the three mothers that p8
identified as the root sūtra of the *Ornament*. The two works divide the same
material by different keys: the *Ornament* by eight abhisamayas, the *Gnod
'joms* by three gates. [Jamyang Zhepa states the same three gates and eleven
rounds](quote:q1101) in the ཕར་ཕྱིན་མཐའ་དཔྱོད་, so the formulation is common
ground between Sera Jey and Gomang.

## 2 · The first gate — the condensed teaching

> **དེ་ལ་སྒོ་གསུམ་ནི། གླེང་གཞིའི་སྐབས་ཕྱེ་བའི་རྗེས་ལ་ཤཱ་རིའི་བུ་ལ། ཞེས་པ་ནས་བརྩོན་པར་བྱའོ། ། ཞེས་པའི་བར་གྱིས་མགོ་སྨོས་པས་གོ་བ་རྣམས་ལ་གང་གིས་གང་གི་ཕྱིར་གང་ལ་ཇི་ལྟར་སློབ་ཚུལ་བཞི་སྟོན་པ་བསྡུས་ཏེ་བསྟན་པའི་སྒོ།**
>
> Of these three gates: after the setting has been opened, the passage from
> "To Śāriputra…" as far as "…should strive" is the gate of teaching in
> condensed form, showing the four modes of training — *by whom*, *for what*,
> *in what*, and *how* — to those who understand from a bare indication.

**The four modes.** གང་གིས (by whom — the practitioner), གང་གི་ཕྱིར (for what
purpose — omniscience), གང་ལ (in what — the perfection of wisdom), ཇི་ལྟར
(how — non-apprehendingly). This is the standard four-fold analysis of the
sūtra's opening injunction, and it is the same sentence cited at p6 as the
first proof that the Prajñāpāramitā is the root sūtra. Here it is being used
structurally rather than evidentially.

## 3 · The second gate — the middling teaching

> **དེ་ནས་རྣམ་མཁྱེན་རྫོགས་པའི་བར་གྱིས་རྣམ་པར་སྤྲོས་ནས་གོ་བ་རྣམས་ལ་རྣམ་མཁྱེན་ལས་བརྩམས་ཏེ། རྣམ་པར་མི་རྟོག་པའི་ཤེར་ཕྱིན་བཟུང་ནས་དོན་དམ་པའི་ཚུལ་དུ་སྟོན་པ་འབྲིང་དུ་བསྟན་པའི་སྒོ།**
>
> Then, up to the completion of omniscience, is the gate of teaching at middling
> length: for those who understand from an elaborated presentation, beginning
> from omniscience, taking up the non-conceptual perfection of wisdom and
> teaching in the manner of the ultimate.

## 4 · The third gate — the extensive teaching

> **དེ་ནས་ཡོངས་སུ་རྫོགས་པའི་བར་གྱིས་དྲངས་དགོས་པའམ། ཚིག་ལ་འཆེལ་བ་རྣམས་ཀྱི་དོན་དུ་ལམ་ཤེས་ལས་བརྩམས་ཏེ། རྣམ་པར་རྟོག་པ་དང་བཅས་པ་དང་རྣམ་པར་མི་རྟོག་པའི་ཤེར་ཕྱིན་བཟུང་ནས་ཀུན་རྫོབ་དང་དོན་དམ་པའི་ཚུལ་དུ་བསྟན་པ་རྒྱས་པར་བཤད་པའི་སྒོའོ། །**
>
> Then, up to the complete conclusion, is the gate of extensive explanation:
> for the sake of those who must be led along, or who cling to the words,
> beginning from the knowledge of paths, taking up both the conceptual and the
> non-conceptual perfection of wisdom, and teaching in the manner of both the
> conventional and the ultimate.

## 5 · Philosophical exposition

**The principle.** The three gates are graded by the *disciple*, not by the
subject matter. The same content is delivered three times over, and what
changes each time is how much elaboration the hearer needs:

| gate | for whom | starting from | what is taken up | taught as |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| condensed | those who understand a bare indication | the injunction to train | — | the four modes |
| middling | those who need elaboration | omniscience | non-conceptual wisdom | the ultimate |
| extensive | those who must be led, or cling to words | knowledge of paths | conceptual and non-conceptual | conventional and ultimate |

Read down the last two columns and the grading is visible: as the audience
needs more, more of the conventional is admitted, and the perfection of wisdom
is presented under more aspects. The third gate does not add doctrine; it adds
scaffolding.

**Sera Jey specificity.** The phrase ཚིག་ལ་འཆེལ་བ ("those who cling to the
words") is not dismissive. It names a real pedagogical case — the disciple who
will not accept a summary and requires each term to be worked through. That the
sūtra itself is held to contain a gate built for such a hearer is a point the
debate ground returns to when asking why the mother sūtras repeat.

## 6 · Key terms

- **སྒོ།** — gate, here a mode of exposition rather than a section. The three
  gates overlap in content and differ in manner.
- **གླེང་གཞི།** — the setting, the narrative frame that opens a sūtra. The
  gates are counted from *after* it.
- **རྣམ་པར་མི་རྟོག་པའི་ཤེར་ཕྱིན།** — the non-conceptual perfection of wisdom:
  the ultimate perfection of wisdom, wisdom as it is in meditative equipoise,
  as against the conceptual (textual and path) perfections of wisdom.
