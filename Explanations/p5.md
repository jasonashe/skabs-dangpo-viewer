# The three Mothers: Sera Jey's own position on the root sūtra

## 1 · Passage & Context

**Source Text:**

> རང་ལུགས་ནི། ཡུམ་རྒྱས་འབྲིང་བསྡུས་གསུམ་པོ་འདི་ཉིད་འདིའི་བཤད་བྱ་རྩ་བའི་མདོ་ཡིན་ཏེ། འགྲེལ་པར། རྒྱལ་བའི་ཡུམ་རྣམ་པ་གསུམ་ཆར་ལས་ཀྱང་བརྗོད་པར་བྱ་བ་ཉིད་དུ་བསྟན་པ། ཞེས་དང་། ལ་ལ་ལས་དོན་གཞན་ལས་ཚིག ། གཞན་ལས་རྣམ་གྲངས་མིང་གིས་ཏེ། ། མཁས་པ་རྣམས་ཀྱིས་ཡུམ་ཀུན་ལས། ། དོན་བསྡུས་དེ་ཙམ་ཤེས་པར་བྱ། ། ཞེས་གསུངས་པའི་ཕྱིར།

**Preceding Section:** p4, which refused two counts of the root sūtra — the
seventeen Mothers-and-Sons of earlier Tibetans, and the nine of Great Ar and
some later scholars — and then disposed of an objection that would have rescued
the second. It ends on the bam po measure of the Eight Thousand. Nothing
positive had yet been asserted.

**Following Section:** p6, which opens གཉིས་པ་ལ་ཁ་ཅིག — "as to the second, some
say" — and turns to the *second* preliminary, how many Indian systems
originate a reading of the sūtra. The first preliminary therefore closes here,
in 295 characters.

**What this paragraph is.** The whole of the first preliminary's positive
answer. Two lines of assertion and two citations, and that is all. After the
length of p3 and p4 the brevity is conspicuous and it is deliberate: everything
that needed arguing was argued negatively, and what remains is to name the
position and show that it is not Chökyi Gyaltsen's invention.

## 3 · Structural Context (Dkar Chag)

- **Abhisamayālaṃkāra location:** indirect but real, and it is the strongest
  thing in this paragraph. The second citation stands in Haribhadra's comment on
  the root verse
  [ཡུལ་ནི་རྣམ་པ་གསུམ་པོ་རྒྱུ། ། སྦྱོར་བ་བཞི་ཡི་བདག་ཉིད་དང་། ། ཆོས་སྐུ་འཕྲིན་ལས་འབྲས་བུ་ནི། ། དོན་བསྡུས་གཞན་ཏེ་རྣམ་པ་གསུམ།](quote:p5-q03)
  — "the three objects are the cause, the four applications are the nature, and
  the dharmakāya with its activity is the fruit: that is a further threefold
  summary of the meaning." Maitreya's "further three" is a summary of the eight
  subjects into cause, practice and fruit. What Haribhadra writes immediately
  before the verse p5 quotes is
  [རྒྱལ་བའི་ཡུམ་རྣམ་པ་གསུམ་གྱི་དོན་བསྡུས་པ་གཞན་རྣམ་པ་གསུམ་སྟེ། དེ་ཁོ་ན་བཞིན་དུ་བཤད་པར་བྱ་བ་ཡིན་ནོ། །](quote:p5-q04)
  — "the condensed meaning **of the three kinds of Conqueror's Mother** is a
  further three kinds; it is to be explained just so." He has read the root
  verse's དོན་བསྡུས as the summary *of the three Mothers*. p5's position is
  therefore not merely supported by Haribhadra but written into his gloss of a
  root verse.
- **Haribhadra's commentary location:** both citations are his, and they stand
  at the two ends of the འགྲེལ་པ་དོན་གསལ།. The first is in the opening section
  where he states the treatise's འབྲེལ་བ — its relation to what it expounds —
  just before the eight-subjects verse. The second is in the closing verses,
  three lines before the colophon. p5 has taken the commentary's first
  statement of its object and its last, and set them side by side.
- **Text location:** the close of the first preliminary,
  རྒྱན་གྱི་བཤད་བྱའི་རྩ་བའི་མདོ་ངོས་གཟུང་བ, and therefore the end of the
  sequence p3–p5.
- **Section type:** རང་ལུགས་བཞག་པ — establishing one's own position, by
  citation rather than by reasoning.
- **Argumentative role:** it closes the first preliminary and licenses the
  second. Once the root sūtra is fixed as the three Mothers, p6 can ask which
  Indian systems expound *that*.

## 4 · Philosophical Exposition & Commentary

**Core meaning.** Two claims are being made at once and it is worth separating
them. The first is **enumerative**: there are three, not seventeen and not
nine. The second is **relational**: the *Ornament*'s subject-matter is drawn
from all three together, so that no one of them is the root sūtra by itself.
The second is the more interesting, and it is the one the citations establish.

**The first citation, in its own place.** In Haribhadra's own wording the lemma
runs
[རྒྱལ་བའི་ཡུམ་རྣམ་པ་གསུམ་ཅར་ལས་བརྗོད་པར་བྱ་བ་ཉིད་དུ་བསྟན་པ](quote:p5-q01) — p5
prints ཆར for his ཅར, an orthographic difference of no consequence, and adds
ཀྱང. The place matters more than the wording. It stands in the passage where
Haribhadra sets out the treatise's འབྲེལ་བ, the relation between expresser and
expressed, immediately before ལུས་རྣམ་གཞག and the eight-subjects verse. A
commentary states its object there or nowhere. So p5 is citing the sentence in
which Haribhadra says what the *Ornament* is a commentary on — which is exactly
what the first preliminary is asking.

**The particle p5 adds, and what Tsongkhapa does with it.** The ཀྱང in
གསུམ་ཆར་ལས་ཀྱང is not idle. Tsongkhapa glosses it:
[ཀྱང་ནི་ནང་ཕན་ཚུན་བསྡུ་བའོ། །](quote:p5-q08) — "the ཀྱང is a mutual
inclusion." That is, the three are being gathered into one another, not merely
listed; the subject-matter is drawn from each *and* they overlap. Reading the
lemma without the particle gives the weaker sense that the *Ornament* draws on
three separate sources. Reading it with the particle, as Tsongkhapa does, gives
the sense p5 needs — one object under three extents.

**Tsongkhapa's gloss supplies p5's own words.** The formula
ཡུམ་རྒྱས་འབྲིང་བསྡུས, with which p5 opens, is the gser phreng's:
[བརྗོད་པར་བྱ་བ་ཉིད་དུ་བསྟན་པ་གང་ཡིན་པ་ནི་འདི་ཡི་བརྗོད་བྱའོ། ། གང་ལས་ན། རྒྱལ་བའི་ཡུམ་རྒྱས་འབྲིང་བསྡུས་པ་རྣམ་པ་གསུམ་ཆར་ལས་སོ། །](quote:p5-q07)
— "what is shown as the very thing to be expressed is the subject-matter of
this treatise. From what? From all three kinds of Conqueror's Mother, extensive,
middling and condensed." Chökyi Gyaltsen's རང་ལུགས is Tsongkhapa's gloss of
Haribhadra's lemma, stated as a thesis. This is worth setting against p4: there
the yig cha borrowed Tsongkhapa's demolition of the counts but stopped before
his own count of four or five; here it takes his gloss word for word. The
selectivity is now visible as a pattern — the gser phreng is followed where it
comments on Haribhadra and departed from where it counts sūtras on its own
authority.

**Gyaltsab Je on the same lemma, and a reading he rejects.** The other of the
two expositions treats the lemma at length. His construal is
[ཐེག་ཆེན་སེམས་བསྐྱེད་ཀྱི་དམིགས་ཡུལ་ཡུམ་བར་མ་བཞིན་དུ་རྒྱས་བསྡུས་གཉིས་ལས་ཀྱང་བསྟན་པས་རྒྱལ་བའི་ཡུམ་རྣམ་པ་གསུམ་ཆར་ལས་ཀྱང་བསྡུས་པ་དང་རྒྱས་པའི་སྒོ་ནས་སེམས་བསྐྱེད་ཀྱི་དམིགས་ཡུལ་ཡང་དག་པར་རྫོགས་པའི་བྱང་ཆུབ་དང་གཞན་གྱི་དོན་བསྟན་ཏེ།](quote:p5-q09)
— the object of the Mahāyāna generation of mind is taught in the extensive and
condensed as it is in the middling, so that from all three, in condensed and
extensive form alike, that object is shown. And then, sharply:
[རྒྱལ་བའི་ཡུམ་རྣམ་པ་གསུམ་ཆར་ལས་ཀྱང་། ཡུམ་གསུམ་ཆར་དང་མི་འགལ་བར་བསྟན་ཅེས་པར་འགྱུར་རོ། ། ཞེས་ཟེར་བ་ནི་ལྟག་ཆོད་དུ་ཤེས་པར་བྱའོ། །](quote:p5-q10)
— those who make the lemma mean "taught **without contradicting** all three
Mothers" are to be known as ལྟག་ཆོད, over-reaching to their own defeat.

That refutation is what stands behind p5's single sentence. The weaker reading
would leave the root sūtra unfixed: a treatise can fail to contradict any number
of texts without drawing its content from them. Gyaltsab's stronger reading —
the content is *drawn from* all three — is what makes the lemma capable of
answering the question p3 posed. p5 does not argue for it, because Gyaltsab
already has.

**Drepung Loseling reads it the same way.** Panchen Sonam Drakpa's yig cha,
which is an འགྲེལ་ཚིག on Gyaltsab, glosses the lemma in Gyaltsab's own terms:
[རྒྱལ་བའི་ཡུམ་རྣམ་པ་གསུམ་ཆར་ལས་ཀྱང་སེམས་བསྐྱེད་ཀྱི་དམིགས་ཡུལ་ཡང་དག་པར་རྫོགས་པའི་བྱང་ཆུབ་དང་གཞན་གྱི་དོན་གཉིས་བསྟན་ཏེ། ཡུམ་བར་མའི་མདོའི་དོན་དང་མི་འགལ་བར་ཏེ་](quote:p5-q11).
There is no Sera Jey / Loseling divergence here, and it is worth saying so
plainly: the houses divide over many things and this is not one of them.

**The second citation, and the four lines p5 does not quote.** The verse is
Haribhadra's, and it is his closing verse — three lines before the phrase
འདི་ནི་སེང་གེ་བཟང་པོས་བྱས, "this was made by Haribhadra." p5 quotes four lines.
The passage continues:

> [ལ་ལ་ལས་དོན་གཞན་ལས་ཚིག གཞན་ལས་རྣམ་གྲངས་མིང་གིས་ཏེ། ། མཁས་པ་རྣམས་ཀྱིས་ཡུམ་ཀུན་ལའང་། ། དོན་བསྡུས་དེ་ཙམ་ཤེས་པར་བྱ། ། དེ་ལྟ་མིན་ན་ཐམས་ཅད་ཀྱི། ། རིམ་པ་ལེགས་པར་བསྡུས་མ་ཡིན། ། དེ་ལ་བསྡུས་སོགས་རབ་དབྱེ་བས། ། ཁྱད་པར་ཡོད་པ་ཁོ་ནར་ཟད། །](quote:p5-q05)
>
> "…otherwise the sequence of them all would not be well condensed. Among them,
> by the division into condensed and so forth, there is a difference and
> nothing more than that."

The last line is the doctrinal ground of the whole position:
ཁྱད་པར་ཡོད་པ་ཁོ་ནར་ཟད — the divisions into extensive, middling and condensed
mark a difference of *extent only*. If that is right, then the three are one
object presented at three lengths, and asking which of them is *the* root sūtra
is a malformed question. p5 stops before this line. A reader who follows the
citation back gets the reason; a reader who takes p5 at face value gets only
the claim. Note also that Haribhadra writes ཡུམ་ཀུན་ལ**འང**་ where p5 has
ཡུམ་ཀུན་ལ**ས**་ — "in all the Mothers" against "from all the Mothers." The
sense p5 needs is the ablative; the witness in this corpus has the locative.

**The commentary p5 leans on is in Ngok's revision.** Its translation colophon
reads
[སླད་ཀྱི་པཎྜི་ཏ་དཔལ་གོ་མི་འཆི་མེད་ལ་སོགས་པ་དང་། ལོ་ཙཱ་བ་དགེ་སློང་བློ་ལྡན་ཤེས་རབ་ཀྱིས་ལེགས་པར་གཏན་ལ་ཕབ་པའོ། །](quote:p5-q06)
— later finalised by Paṇḍita Śrī Gomi Amara and others with the translator
Bhikṣu Loden Sherab. That is Ngok Lotsāwa, whom p2 singled out for a verse of
his own as བཀའ་དྲིན་མཉམ་མེད་པ, unequalled in kindness in this system. The
Tibetan wording of the sentence on which the whole first preliminary rests is
his. The homage in p2 and the citation in p5 are the same debt, once
acknowledged and once used.

**The nomenclature is Indian, not Tibetan.** One might suspect that
རྒྱས་འབྲིང་བསྡུས is a Tibetan scholastic tidying. It is not: Dharmamitra,
introducing the sūtra lemma p3 quoted, writes
[དེ་ལ་རྒྱལ་བའི་ཡུམ་བར་མའི་དབང་དུ་བྱས་ཏེ་ནི།](quote:p5-q02) — "here, with
reference to the **middling** Mother." The middling is named as such in an
Indian ṭīkā, which presupposes the other two.

**A note on the evidence.** This is the best-supported paragraph of the five.
Both citations are traced to their places in a tier-3 Indian commentary; both of
the tier-2 expositions the yig cha exists to clarify gloss the first of them at
length and agree; the tier-4 root text and a tier-4 Indian ṭīkā corroborate the
frame; and the tier-5 Drepung witnesses concur. What the paragraph does *not*
have is any argument of its own, and that is the honest description of it: p5
is a citation, not a demonstration.

## 5 · Bones of Contention 🦴

**What is disputed?** Given that the root sūtra is the three Mothers, two things
remain open, and p5 settles neither.

**First: which sūtras are the three?** p5 names none of them. The extensive and
middling are not in doubt — the Hundred Thousand and the Twenty-five Thousand.
The condensed is:

- **Some (ཁ་ཅིག, reported by Tsongkhapa):** the Eighteen Thousand, on the
  ground that certain passages are absent from the Eight Thousand.
- **Je Tsongkhapa:** the Eight Thousand —
  [ཁ་ཅིག་བརྒྱད་སྟོང་པ་ན་མདོ་འདི་དག་མེད་པས་བསྡུས་པ་ནི་ཁྲི་བརྒྱད་སྟོང་པ་ལ་བྱའོ་ཞེས་ཟེར་ཡང་རྟོགས་དཀར་བཤད་པ་ལྟར་བརྒྱད་སྟོང་པ་ལ་བྱ་སྟེ།](quote:p5-q08),
  "although some say the condensed should be taken as the Eighteen Thousand
  because these sūtra passages are missing in the Eight Thousand, it should be
  taken as the Eight Thousand, as explained in the *Rtogs dkar*." I have not
  been able to identify the work abbreviated རྟོགས་དཀར in this corpus, and I do
  not guess.
- **Sera Jey, here:** silent.

The silence is defensible — the question belongs to the enumeration p4 has just
finished refusing, and re-opening it would undo the paragraph's economy — but it
leaves a debater who is asked "which is the condensed Mother?" with nothing from
p5 and a divided answer from the gser phreng.

**Second: three, or four or five?** Tsongkhapa's own count, reached in p4's
source by a different route — from the twelve Indian commentaries that match
sūtra to treatise — was four or five. p5 says three and does not mention it.
Two readings are available and the text does not choose between them.

- The counts answer different questions. Tsongkhapa is counting *sūtras that
  can be matched* with the *Ornament*'s stages; p5 is naming the *object* of
  the treatise as its commentator states it. A text can be matchable without
  being what the treatise is about.
- Or they genuinely differ, and Sera Jey is preferring the commentator's
  statement of the object to the master's inference from the commentarial
  record.

I incline to the first, because p5's warrant is a lemma about what is
བརྗོད་པར་བྱ་བ, the thing to be expressed, and Tsongkhapa's warrant is
སྦྱར་རུང, admitting of being matched; those are not the same predicate. But the
yig cha does not say this, and presenting the reconciliation as settled would be
overstating what the paragraph contains.

**Who argues what, in sum?**

- **Haribhadra:** the subject-matter is shown from all three Mothers, and their
  differences are of extent only ([q01](quote:p5-q01), [q05](quote:p5-q05)).
- **Je Tsongkhapa:** the same, glossed word by word, with ཀྱང read as mutual
  inclusion ([q07](quote:p5-q07), [q08](quote:p5-q08)) — but four or five root
  sūtras on his own count.
- **Gyaltsab Je:** the same, with the weaker "not contradicting" reading
  explicitly refused ([q09](quote:p5-q09), [q10](quote:p5-q10)).
- **Drepung Loseling — Panchen Sonam Drakpa:** Gyaltsab's reading, restated
  ([q11](quote:p5-q11)).
- **Drepung Gomang — Jamyang Zhepa:** three Mothers, reached from Haribhadra's
  maṅgala instead ([q12](quote:p5-q12)).
- **Sera Jey — Jetsun Chökyi Gyaltsen:** three Mothers, and just these.

**Why does it matter?** Because it fixes what "the sūtra" means for the rest of
the volume. When a later paragraph argues from མདོ་ལས, the sūtra in question is
one of three texts, and a passage found in only one of them is still evidence
about the *Ornament*'s subject-matter — which it would not be if the three were
three different objects. That consequence is carried entirely by Haribhadra's
line about difference of extent, and it is the line p5 leaves out.

**Resolution.** Sera Jey holds the three Mothers, on the authority of the
commentator whose statement of the object both Gelug expositions endorse. The
position is not distinctive to Sera Jey — Gomang and Loseling hold it too — and
saying so is a real finding rather than a concession. What *is* distinctive is
the route: p4 clears the field by refutation and p5 occupies it by citation,
where Gomang simply reads the position off Haribhadra's opening verse without
refuting anyone.

## 6 · Key Terminology & Technical Concepts

- **རང་ལུགས།** — one's own system, as against གཞན་ལུགས.
  - **How understood here:** a formal marker, not a claim to originality.
    Everything in the paragraph is quotation; what རང་ལུགས signals is that the
    refutations are over and what follows is to be held, not reported. A
    Sera Jey monk reading རང་ལུགས་ནི should expect assertion and should notice
    when, as here, the assertion is entirely borrowed.

- **ཡུམ་རྒྱས་འབྲིང་བསྡུས།** — the extensive, middling and condensed Mothers.
  - **How understood here:** three *extents* of one object, not three objects.
    That is the whole weight of the position, and it rests on Haribhadra's
    ཁྱད་པར་ཡོད་པ་ཁོ་ནར་ཟད ([q05](quote:p5-q05)) rather than on anything p5 says.
  - **Debates:** which sūtras fill the three slots — see section 5. The
    extensive and middling are secure; the condensed is disputed between the
    Eight Thousand and the Eighteen Thousand.

- **བརྗོད་པར་བྱ་བ།** — that which is to be expressed; the subject-matter.
  - **Sanskrit equivalent:** *abhidheya*.
  - **How understood here:** one of the four preliminaries an Indian commentary
    states about a treatise (subject-matter, purpose, ultimate purpose,
    relation). Haribhadra's lemma answers the *abhidheya* question, and that is
    why it can settle the first preliminary: p3 asked what the *Ornament* is
    about, and this is the sentence in which its commentator says.
  - **Debates:** Gyaltsab's ལྟག་ཆོད charge ([q10](quote:p5-q10)) is precisely
    about how strong the ལས is — whether the subject-matter is *drawn from* the
    three or merely *consistent with* them.

- **དོན་བསྡུས།** — the condensed meaning.
  - **How understood here:** used twice over, and the two uses are what the
    paragraph's second citation depends on. In the root verse it is the
    threefold summary of the eight subjects into cause, practice and fruit
    ([q03](quote:p5-q03)); in Haribhadra's gloss it is the condensed meaning of
    the three Mothers ([q04](quote:p5-q04)). He allows the one word to carry
    both, and the alignment is the reason the *Ornament*'s architecture can be
    said to be the Mothers' meaning summarised.

- **ཀྱང་།** in གསུམ་ཆར་ལས་ཀྱང།
  - **How understood here:** not the ordinary concessive. Tsongkhapa reads it as
    ནང་ཕན་ཚུན་བསྡུ་བ, mutual gathering — the three are being drawn into one
    another. This is the sort of particle a debater passes over and should not:
    without it the lemma yields three sources, with it one object.
